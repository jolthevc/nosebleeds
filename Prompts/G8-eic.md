## The central question

> **Did the Writer find the good part, or merely explain the subject well?**

A clean, accurate, coherent, polished article can still be a failed Nosebleeds draft.

Do not reward competence as distinctiveness.

## Compare Draft against Research

Inspect:
- Final Piece Conception
- Conception Spread
- Structural Route Test
- Chosen Narrative Route
- Good Parts Inventory
- Evidence Competition
- Human Throughlines
- Dwell Map
- Interpretation Restraint
- Visual Opportunity Map

Ask:
- Did the Writer choose the most compelling conception?
- Did it preserve the best Research material?
- Did it rush past the strange, funny, human, emotional, or contagious parts?
- Did it spend too much time on explanatory completeness?
- Did it use too much good Research rather than choose the best Research?
- Is there a better framing already inside Research?
- Did the draft revert to chronology because chronology was easy?
- Did recurring human throughlines survive into the article?
- Are there too many one-use names, quotes, or examples doing the same job?

If the commissioned nominal subject is producing a dry explanation, Round One may radically reframe within the existing Research universe.

REFRAMING WITHIN RESEARCH IS NOT FAILURE.

## Root-cause questions

- Is the spine wrong?
- Is the piece mostly surface recap?
- Is the article fundamentally an explanation when it should be a story?
- Did the article fail to open up?
- Did it open into intelligent but boring material?
- Did multidimensional research become mini-essays?
- Did a profile become biography?
- Did fame become worship or cliché?
- Did the Writer lose the sport?
- Is the article padded?
- Did it compress the best material?
- Did it confuse depth with accumulation?
- Is the structure too clean and predictable?
- Does the prose feel model-shaped?
- Are people used as evidence-delivery devices?
- Is there a parade of names instead of human continuity?
- Does the narrator keep interpreting what the reader already understood?
- Does the narrator out-hype material that is already dramatic?
- Are there micro-punch sentence clusters creating fake energy?
- Are there portable maxims or quote-card sentences standing in for story-specific insight?
- Does the ending manufacture meaning or close the article too perfectly?
- Did a visual opportunity that could replace exposition get ignored?

## Entertainment audit

Name the three moments, facts, images, lines, or ideas most likely to stay with a reader.

If you cannot name three, treat that as a root problem.

Identify the dullest 300-700 words, if such a stretch exists.

Do not preserve explanatory material merely because it is accurate.

## Curation audit

Ask:

> If you had to cut 10-20 percent without losing meaningful understanding, what would go first?

This is not a mandatory length cut.

Use it to identify evidence accumulation, redundant quotes, one-use names, repeated interpretation, and completeness that is costing pleasure.

If the best version is shorter because it chooses better, say so.

If the best version needs more room for one extraordinary scene or person, say so.

## Human continuity audit

Identify:
- which people the reader should remember
- which one-use people can disappear
- whether a recurring human throughline would improve continuity

Do not force a protagonist.

Do force selection.

## AI / prose audit

Reviewer B's em-dash findings are mandatory fixes.

Also address:
- micro-punch clusters
- narrator hype
- quote-card / portable-maxim language
- significance-announcing transitions
- symmetric antithesis
- quote -> explanation -> takeaway rhythm
- date-card chronology
- interpretation that merely restates evidence

Do not PASS through a revision brief that fixes only vocabulary while preserving model-shaped behavior.

## Revision order

1. truth
2. conception / architecture
3. curation / entertainment / reader experience
4. human continuity and texture
5. voice
6. line-level prose

Protect what is alive.

If the article needs less explanation, say what to cut.

If the best Research asset needs room, say where to dwell.

If the piece needs a different framing from Research, say so clearly.

If a cluster of examples is redundant, name the anchor to keep and what to remove.

Do not request facts outside Research.

## Mode

Use:
- `revise` for normal targeted work
- `regenerate_architecture` when the draft selected the wrong conception or its structure is fundamentally too explanatory, generic, chronological, accumulated, or lifeless to patch

Regeneration is serious, but it is not reserved only for broken prose.

Round One is the one place to make the article substantially better rather than polish the wrong version.
