# Nosebleeds Narrative Architectures

**Version 1.2 - Batch Three - Covers Build Mandate Deliverable 08 (Narrative Architecture System).**  
**Inherits:** Editorial OS v1.0, Brand Bible v1.3, Voice Bible v0.8, Ideation and Selection Standard v1.2, Research Standard v1.2, Master Scoring Rubric v1.2. Binds by reference.

There is no universal Nosebleeds article structure. There is a library of shapes, a rule for choosing one, and one doctrine every shape obeys: **hook early, hinge later**. The Story Architecture role picks a shape from the Research Packet, adapts it, and hands the writer a beat plan.

The shape is scaffolding. The reader must never see it.

## 1. How a shape is chosen

The Story Architecture role reads the Research Packet and answers, in order:

1. **What is the hook fact, and is it the outcome?** If the outcome is the hook, the shape states it early and finds its tension elsewhere. If the outcome is the payoff, the shape preserves it until the story reaches it.
2. **Where is the narrative hinge, and how long is it?** A single moment wants a shape that slows at one point. A bounded sequence wants more than one beat of change. The architecture must locate the hinge without forcing a diffuse story into one date.
3. **Who or what carries it?** One person, two people in opposition, a documented collective, or an object. This determines the spine. A collective is valid only when it is genuinely the human subject, not a substitute for a missing protagonist.
4. **What register did the packet expect, and does the evidence support it?** The architecture confirms or changes the register and records why.
5. **What does the Belief File permit?** Full, reduced, thin, skipped, or inevitability mode. A full Belief File supports reconstruction. Inevitability mode forbids fake suspense. A thin file may force a simpler shape, shorter format, or reframe.
6. **What is the ending?** The packet's ending candidates are ranked. The architecture chooses one and names a fallback. A piece whose ending is still unknown at architecture is not ready to draft.
7. **What does the story need visually?** The architecture identifies visual functions and placements, not final images. It creates visual needs that the Visual Research role resolves after architecture.

The output is a beat plan, not an outline of sections. Beats are what the reader experiences. Sections are one possible way the writer gets there.

## 2. The shapes

Fourteen core shapes follow. Each lists when it works, when it fails, its natural hook, hinge, ending, and visual opportunities. Shapes may combine. Most Core Stories use one primary shape with one borrowed move.

### 2.1 Chronological reconstruction

Events proceed in order from a deliberately chosen starting point, with the hinge arriving after enough road has accumulated to make it matter. There is no fixed percentage.

**Works when:** the sequence itself contains discovery; the reader does not already know the order; the Belief File is strong enough that each step changes what people reasonably believed.

**Fails when:** the reader knows the outcome and the sequence has no surprises; it becomes history rather than story; the chosen start is childhood merely because childhood came first.

**Hook:** the strangest or most consequential fact near the hinge, placed early, then a controlled rewind to the chosen start.

**Hinge:** the decisive moment or bounded sequence, slowed enough to register.

**Ending:** the aftermath's most concrete artifact, action, or later fact.

**Visual opportunities:** timeline, contemporaneous front page, box score or result, object from the hinge.

### 2.2 Cold open and rewind

Begin inside the hinge moment or just before it, stop before the relevant payoff, go back to how everyone got there, return to the moment, and finish it.

**Works when:** the moment is vivid and specific, the outcome is not the hook, and the road to it contains discoveries of its own.

**Fails when:** the rewind becomes a context dump; the return merely restates the opening; the hinge scene is not actually the strongest scene in the piece.

**Hook:** the moment, cut off before the relevant payoff.

**Hinge:** the return, completed.

**Ending:** usually a later consequence, person, object, or fact rather than the hinge scene already used.

**Visual opportunities:** photograph from the moment, placed at the return rather than the opening; document that explains the road.

### 2.3 Object-led

An object such as a shoe, suit, bike, letter, seat, certificate, bat, ticket, or piece of equipment is the spine. The story is what happened to and around it.

**Works when:** the object is real, documentable, photographable or reconstructable, and its meaning changed. Particularly useful for Unknown Part of a Known Thing stories.

**Fails when:** the object is only a metaphor; the story repeatedly leaves the object for the people and never returns; the object is interesting but no human consequence attaches to it.

**Hook:** the object doing something it should not, or carrying a meaning the reader does not expect.

**Hinge:** the moment the object's meaning changed, such as the ban, sale, ruling, letter, failure, or breakthrough.

**Ending:** where the object, its rule, or its consequence is now.

**Visual opportunities:** object as hero, underlying documents, object in use, diagram of what made it different.

### 2.4 Mystery

A real question is posed early and the piece assembles evidence toward an answer or an honest non-answer.

**Works when:** the record is genuinely disputed, the mythology audit found a gap, or apparently compatible facts do not add up.

**Fails when:** the question is rhetorical; the answer was obvious in the first search result; the piece hides a basic fact merely to keep the device alive.

**Hook:** a verified fact or contradiction that does not add up.

**Hinge:** the evidence that resolves, narrows, or reframes the question.

**Ending:** the answer, or the precise shape of what cannot honestly be known.

**Visual opportunities:** conflicting documents, source-chain artifact, record that resolves the dispute.

### 2.5 Rivalry

Two people, teams, or ideas in opposition form an alternating spine. The hinge is a meeting, answer, reversal, or decisive comparison.

**Works when:** both sides are documented as real agents rather than one side serving as a foil; their difference is the story; the reader can see the contrast in specific encounters, decisions, or styles.

**Fails when:** the rivalry is asserted rather than shown; one side exists only to decorate the profile of the other; the piece has to manufacture symmetry.

**Hook:** an encounter, score, quote, decision, or fact that makes the opposition visible.

**Hinge:** the decisive meeting or the moment one side answered the other.

**Ending:** the two sides afterward, together or apart, in one concrete image or fact.

**Visual opportunities:** contemporaneous side-by-side images, head-to-head record, decisive moment, documents from both sides.

### 2.6 Institutional decision

A league, city, network, governing body, court, or organization decides. The piece follows the decision to the person or documented collective that lived under it.

**Works when:** there is a document, vote, memo, letter, ruling, rule, or other tangible decision; there is a person or genuine collective on the receiving end; the decision can be understood as it looked at the time before the consequences were obvious.

**Fails when:** it becomes process; the human consequence appears only in the conclusion; the collective is invoked vaguely because no real carrier exists; the piece editorializes before showing the record.

**Hook:** the decision stated flat, preferably in its own language when that wording matters.

**Hinge:** the moment the decision met reality.

**Ending:** the person or collective consequence, not the institution congratulating itself.

**Visual opportunities:** decision document, receiving person or collective, before-and-after measure.

### 2.7 Comeback

A fall, a period out, and a return. The oldest sports shape and one of the easiest to write badly.

**Works when:** the fall is specific and documented; the return was genuinely uncertain or meaningfully difficult; the piece can resist the motivational register.

**Fails when:** it becomes a lesson; the fall is summarized while the triumph gets all the scenes; the return is treated as inevitable; the person's later inspirational framing becomes the article's framing.

**Hook:** the fall at its lowest specific point, such as a number, scene, decision, or headline from the time.

**Hinge:** the return's meaningful test, told without importing the final success too early.

**Ending:** a small fact from afterward, an ordinary day, or the next problem beginning.

**Visual opportunities:** contemporaneous material from the fall, return photography, result or document from the test.

### 2.8 Wrong ending

The result feels wrong, the call is disputed, the record refuses to settle, or the consequence outlives the event. This is an argument shape, not a neutrality requirement.

**Works when:** the record contains real evidence and the aftermath is itself a story. The article can present competing evidence honestly before reaching any judgment.

**Fails when:** the narrator picks a side before showing the record; the grievance substitutes for evidence; the piece pretends both sides are equally strong when they are not.

**Hook:** the result stated flat, plus the fact that makes the result difficult to accept or understand.

**Hinge:** the decisive act, call, replay, ruling, refusal, or document.

**Ending:** the dispute's most concrete artifact or consequence.

**Judgment rule:** Nosebleeds does not manufacture false neutrality. If the evidence ultimately supports a judgment, the article may make it after the record has been shown fairly. But judgment is permitted, not required. The argument mechanism is often part of the story's contagion, and some Wrong Ending pieces are stronger when the evidence leaves the reader with an honest unresolved question. Architecture records the intended posture, and the EIC decides in review whether an evidence-backed judgment or an open question better serves the specific piece.

**Visual opportunities:** frame, clock, document, conflicting contemporaneous coverage, enduring artifact.

### 2.9 Fan story

What people did because they cared. This is the shape with the least narrator.

**Works when:** the act is documented, specific, and collective, inherited, or personally concrete; the piece can stay close to names, objects, places, and actions.

**Fails when:** the article explains what the act means after the act already showed it; it becomes a survey; fans are reduced to quotes about feelings rather than shown doing something.

**Hook:** the act, seen.

**Hinge:** often the game or event the act responds to, told briefly and second; sometimes the fan act itself.

**Ending:** one name, one object, one fact, one last action.

**Visual opportunities:** the act photographed, object, wall, seat, place, program, memorial, letter.

### 2.10 Comedic escalation

Things get worse, stranger, or more official in a sequence, and each escalation is a verified fact.

**Works when:** the packet contains enough distinct verified escalations to sustain the target length without padding; the people or institution involved took the situation seriously enough for the facts to create their own comedy; any real harm can be handled honestly without turning the victim into the joke.

**Fails when:** the writer adds jokes; the sequence is padded to hit a count; the piece becomes a listicle in prose; a fan, victim, or injured person becomes the target.

**Hook:** the single most absurd verified fact, stated flat and early.

**Hinge:** the point of no return, such as the forfeit, ban, arrest, rule, decision, or official intervention.

**Ending:** the driest fitting fact available, often an official statement, result, or administrative consequence.

**Visual opportunities:** poster, program, newspaper headline, official notice, scoreboard, object.

### 2.11 Myth versus record

The standard telling, then the documents, then what the record can actually support, with affection for why the myth survived.

**Works when:** the myth audit found a real gap, mutation, contradiction, or resolving document; the record is at least as interesting as the legend.

**Fails when:** it becomes debunking for sport; the article sneers at people who believed the story; the actual record is thinner and less meaningful than the myth and there is no larger story in that gap.

**Hook:** tell the myth as the myth people came to know, with its epistemic status clear. The verified hook is the existence of the legend, the document that challenges it, or the contradiction in the record. The prose never states the legend flat merely because it is famous.

**Hinge:** the document, source-chain break, or evidence that changes the reader's understanding.

**Ending:** the myth and record held together in one fact, image, or sentence, or the concrete reason the myth won.

**Visual opportunities:** resolving document, myth's own artifact, source chain, ad, poster, letter.

### 2.12 Unknown part of a known thing

Start with the thing everyone knows, stated briefly, and go underneath.

**Works when:** recognition is instant; the underneath is verified or correctly attributed; the unknown part opens onto a real story with a hinge and human consequence.

**Fails when:** the known part takes too long; the underneath is trivia; the article merely appends an obscure fact to a famous event.

**Hook:** the known thing and the unknown fact in the same sentence or paragraph when possible.

**Hinge:** the moment underneath, such as the benching, letter, equipment decision, measuring failure, ruling, or human choice.

**Ending:** back at the known thing, now changed by one concrete fact.

**Visual opportunities:** famous image followed by unfamiliar document, object, or alternate perspective.

### 2.13 Obsession

A life or effort organized around a margin. This is the shape with the most time in it.

**Works when:** the margin is concrete and often quantifiable; the ordinary work is documented; years, repetitions, schedules, logs, or small decisions build pressure.

**Fails when:** it becomes a profile; the person's own inspirational framing becomes the narrator's; the margin remains abstract.

**Hook:** the ratio, margin, repeated action, or impossible-looking number.

**Hinge:** the moment the margin was tested.

**Ending:** the ordinary day after, the next cycle beginning, or another concrete fact that restores scale.

**Visual opportunities:** timing sheet, training log, schedule, margin diagram, equipment.

### 2.14 Business meets game

Money, media, law, ownership, or distribution changed what a fan, athlete, team, or game experienced. This is the shape most at risk of leaving orbit.

**Works when:** the piece opens and closes on the sport-side consequence; the mechanism is explained only when it becomes plot; the packet has a person or documented collective on the affected side and a real decision-maker, institution, or mechanism on the other.

**Fails when:** it opens on the industry; explanation arrives before the reader has a question; the fan or athlete appears only as a conclusion; it reads like a memo with a jersey on.

**Hook:** the fan-side or game-side fact.

**Hinge:** the decision, contract, ruling, media choice, ownership act, or business mechanism meeting the sport.

**Ending:** the fan, athlete, team, or game, not the spreadsheet.

**Visual opportunities:** television listing, certificate, contract, letter, ticket, before-and-after schedule, relevant object. Never lead with a chart before the reader has a reason to want one.

## 3. Moves that any shape may borrow

### The silence before

A short pause immediately before the hinge in which the clock, wait, room, official, tape, or other concrete detail carries the tension.

Use sparingly. Repetition usually destroys the effect.

### The other broadcast

The opposing side's, foreign press's, or otherwise distinct contemporaneous account of the same moment. It restores uncertainty and often supplies discovery cheaply.

### The document, quoted

A letter, memo, rulebook line, ruling, or other document in its own words, with quotation length governed by the Research Standard. Particularly useful in Bite and Myth-versus-Record stories.

### The number, alone

A verified figure given enough space to register when the number itself is the fact. Do not turn this into a required one-sentence paragraph.

### The reaction shot

After the hinge, show what the relevant human environment did: crowd, bench, announcer, opponent, parent, office, family, street, or room. Then trust the reaction.

### The later fact

One fact from years or months after the hinge that recontextualizes without requiring a summary paragraph.

## 4. The beat plan: Story Architecture output

Every beat's pull is checked later by the Momentum and Structure Editor. A beat with no reason to continue is rejected at architecture rather than discovered at review.

```text
architecture:
  brief_id
  packet_version
  architecture_version

  shape_primary
  shape_borrowed_moves: []

  register_confirmed:
    value
    change_reason: optional

  format_confirmed:
    quick | core | big

  target_words:
    article-level target only

  hook_fact_placement:
    sentence_1 | paragraph_1 | early_exception
    outcome_is_hook: yes | no

  spine:
    type: person | two_persons | collective | object
    named
    collective_stakes_justification: required if collective

  hero_need:
    visual_need_id
    function: prove | show | explain
    what_needed
    preferred_real: yes | no
    research_lead_ids: []

  beats:
    - beat_id
      purpose:
        hook | road | complication | silence | hinge |
        reaction | aftermath | later_fact | ending

      content:
        what happens in this beat, from the packet, with claim_ids

      relative_weight:
        light | medium | heavy

      pull:
        what makes the reader want the next beat
        required for every beat except the last

      register_note:
        only when the register changes

      hindsight_hazards_active: []

      visual_need:
        none |
        {
          visual_need_id,
          function: prove | show | explain,
          what_needed,
          research_lead_ids: []
        }

      explanation_points:
        [ rule_or_mechanism,
          beat_immediately_before_it_becomes_plot,
          maximum_explanation_scope ]

  ending_chosen:
    packet_candidate_id
    function
    fallback_candidate_id

  wrong_ending_posture:
    not_applicable | evidence_backed_judgment | honest_open_question
    rationale: required when applicable

  calibration_passage_ids:
    two or three register-matched passages to be served to the writer

  protected_intent:
    facts: []
    relationships: []
    beat_functions: []
    discovery: []
    ending_function: []

  risks:
    [ leaving_orbit | history_drift | inspirational_drift |
      fake_suspense | joke_padding | protagonist_missing |
      collective_as_loophole | explanation_overload ]
```

The architecture protects intent, not prose that does not yet exist. Sentence-level protection begins only after a draft exists.

`relative_weight` communicates proportion without giving the drafting model a hidden word-count template. Overall article length remains a target, not a quota.

## 5. Architecture prohibitions

- No beat may contain a claim not in the Research Packet.
- No beat may be named merely "background" or "context." Context is distributed into the beats that need it, at the point the reader has the question.
- No shape may begin with biography, a generalization about sports, or an abstract thesis unless the verified hook itself requires that form, which should be rare and explicitly justified.
- No shape may withhold the hook fact merely to protect an outcome. Reveal the premise; preserve the uncertainty that matters.
- In inevitability mode, no shape may create pull through manufactured doubt.
- A collective may not be used merely because the architecture cannot find a human carrier.
- The ending is chosen before drafting. `ending_chosen: tbd` is invalid.
- Visual architecture identifies a need, function, and placement. It may not invent a URL, credit, photographer, candidate, or image that Visual Research has not actually found or specified as a constructed fallback.
