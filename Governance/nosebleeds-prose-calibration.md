# NOSEBLEEDS
## Prose Calibration v1.0
### Alive, human, varied, and unselfconscious

**Status:** Positive runtime calibration for G4, G4.5, G6, G9, G10, and selected EIC work.

---

# 0. THIS IS CALIBRATION, NOT A STYLE TEMPLATE

The examples below demonstrate qualities of prose.

Do not copy wording, jokes, sentence openings, sentence lengths, connective patterns, metaphors, asides, paragraph shapes, cadence, or subject matter.

Do not imitate a sample so closely that a reader could recognize its skeleton.

Transfer the principles: thoughts connect naturally, sentences breathe without becoming breathless, voice has personality without performing personality, humor comes from noticing, concrete material carries the story, ordinary sentences coexist with colorful ones, sentence lengths and structures vary, and the narrator can lean in and then get out of the way.

If every paragraph starts sounding like these examples, you are overfitting.

---

# 1. THE TARGET FEEL

Imagine a smart sports fan who has done far more reading than the person sitting across from them, yet is telling the story because it is too good not to share.

The voice can feel like a friend giving an aside, circling back to a detail, or noticing something ridiculous.

It should still be edited magazine prose.

The goal is not casual transcription.

The goal is **human shape**.

---

# 2. CONNECT THOUGHTS NATURALLY

AI prose often treats every fact as a separate unit.

Human prose frequently connects related thoughts with `and`, `but`, `yet`, `or`, `while`, `because`, `even as`, `which`, and `when`.

Connectors can make prose warmer and more fluid. Do not use them mechanically, and do not turn every sentence into a chain of clauses.

## Flat

> Biasone kept pushing the idea. Ferris worked through the arithmetic. The owners were skeptical. The league had tried other fixes.

## Better instinct

> In Syracuse, Biasone kept selling the idea while Ferris worked the numbers, and by then the league had tried enough clever little fixes to know how this usually ended: coaches nodded, the rulebook changed, and somebody found a new way around it.

Why it works:

- related facts become one movement
- the prepositional opening places us somewhere
- the connector carries thought rather than stacking facts
- there is a dry aside without a joke setup

Do not copy the syntax.

---

# 3. AVOID BOTH STACCATO AND BREATHLESSNESS

There are two bad extremes.

## Too clipped

> The whistles never stopped. Players walked to the line. Shot. Missed. Walked back.

## Too breathless

> With the fourth quarter stretching toward forty-five real minutes and whistles arriving every few seconds while substitutions piled up and players walked back and forth to the line and the broadcast window kept shrinking, the game became an endless sequence of stoppages that left everyone waiting for basketball to happen again.

## Better instinct

> By the fourth quarter, the game had been going for nearly forty-five real minutes, and another whistle seemed to arrive before the last free throw had finished mattering. Players changed, coaches argued, the teams walked back to the line, and somewhere inside all of that a one-point playoff game was still trying to finish.

Why it works:

- sentence lengths vary
- connectors create flow
- the prose does not pant
- the final phrase adds personality without announcing a lesson

A sentence can carry two linked ideas. It does not need to carry six.

---

# 4. PREPOSITIONAL OPENINGS CAN ADD COLOR

Useful openings include:

- In Syracuse,
- By the fourth quarter,
- On paper,
- For Pollard,
- With Mikan waiting near the lane,
- After three years of this,
- From the owners' side,
- Inside Boston Garden,

They can provide place, time, perspective, or emotional orientation before the main clause arrives.

Do not use one at the start of every paragraph.

## Flat

> Pollard objected to the clock because he believed it reduced strategic sophistication.

## Better instinct

> For Jim Pollard, the old patience was part of the intelligence of basketball. If a team had the ball and a good reason not to give it back, why should a buzzer suddenly get a vote?

The rhetorical question works here because it inhabits his objection. Do not turn rhetorical questions into a transition habit.

---

# 5. LET SENTENCE ENDINGS CARRY TEXTURE

Not every sentence should end on the informational payload.

Sometimes a final phrase can add human color.

## Flat

> Fort Wayne attempted thirteen field goals, an extremely low total.

## Better instinct

> Fort Wayne attempted thirteen field goals all night, the sort of number that now looks less like an NBA box score than a typo somebody forgot to fix.

The aside is earned by the fact.

Do not add a clever tail to every sentence.

---

# 6. PERSONALITY WITHOUT PERFORMANCE

The narrator may notice, smile, doubt, or gently tease the situation.

The narrator should never sound desperate to entertain.

## Lifeless

> In January 1954, owners implemented an honor system to reduce intentional fouling.

## Better instinct

> By January, the league was down to the cheapest fix available: asking everyone to knock it off. Coaches more or less agreed, and then the games started counting again.

This is light jest. It is not contemptuous. It does not make the people in the story stupid.

The joke comes from the mismatch between competitive incentives and polite requests.

---

# 7. HUMOR COMES FROM NOTICING

Good Nosebleeds humor can sound like:

> The famous number was not handed down on stone tablets. Biasone and Ferris looked at games they liked, counted roughly 120 shots, divided 2,880 by 120, and got 24. It was less a natural law than a very confident answer to the question, "How much basketball would we like these people to play?"

The humor lives inside the history.

Bad humor would mock old players for being old-fashioned, ridicule fans, turn tragedy into a bit, make a joke every paragraph, or use meme language to prove cultural fluency.

Different stories require different temperatures.

---

# 8. PEOPLE SHOULD SOUND LIKE PEOPLE, NOT SOURCES

## Source-shaped

> Jim Pollard objected to the shot clock, arguing that it eliminated strategic intelligence.

## Better instinct

> Pollard hated it. To him, taking your time was part of knowing how to play, and forcing a team to throw something toward the rim because a buzzer was getting impatient sounded suspiciously like making basketball dumber in the name of making it faster.

The sentence has character, but it remains tied to his documented position.

Do not invent emotions or reactions that Research does not support.

---

# 9. DO NOT EXPLAIN THE GREAT FACT TWICE

## Overwritten

> DuMont's connection ended before the game finished. Professional basketball had finally secured a national television platform, only for its own structural flaws to consume the broadcast.

## Better instinct

> With about ten seconds left in a one-point playoff game, DuMont's connection ended. The audience never saw the finish.

Stop.

The fact already contains the meaning.

A good next paragraph may move somewhere else.

---

# 10. MECHANISM IN HUMAN LANGUAGE

## Research-memo prose

> The freeze-foul-counterfoul mechanism created an inefficient late-game equilibrium.

## Better instinct

> If you were behind, you needed the ball, so you fouled. If you were ahead, you could make a free throw and foul right back before the other team had a chance to score two. Close games could turn into two teams escorting each other from one foul line to the other.

Explain the thing the way an intelligent friend would explain it.

Do not dumb it down.

Do not preserve the vocabulary of the Research memo merely because it is precise.

---

# 11. ORDINARY SENTENCES ARE PART OF GOOD WRITING

Not every line needs color.

Example:

> Biasone kept pushing the idea. Ferris worked through the arithmetic, and the owners had already spent years watching smaller fixes fail.

That sentence is doing useful work.

The next sentence can be more vivid. The one after that can be plain again.

Variation creates life.

---

# 12. ASIDES SHOULD FEEL INCIDENTAL

A good aside sounds discovered in the telling.

> The clock was tested before anyone had built a proper clock for it. A man stood on the sideline with a stopwatch and called out the seconds, which is a wonderfully literal way to beta-test the future of professional basketball.

The aside adds character.

Do not write an aside merely because the paragraph has gone too long without a joke.

---

# 13. SERIOUS STORIES NEED A DIFFERENT TEMPERATURE

Nosebleeds is not always amused.

For grief, injury, death, injustice, or genuine vulnerability, warmth and restraint matter more than jest.

## Too polished

> The mistake became a testament to sportsmanship and the redemptive power of forgiveness.

## Better instinct

> Joyce knew almost immediately what he had done. Galarraga knew too, and somehow the two men spent the next day making the mistake easier on each other instead of harder.

The humanity is in the behavior.

No moral needs to be announced.

---

# 14. BUSINESS AND STRATEGY SHOULD STILL SOUND LIKE SPORTS

## Trade prose

> The Rams apply a differentiated capital-allocation framework based on positional scarcity.

## Better instinct

> The Rams are perfectly willing to pay a fortune for one player and replace another every spring, which sounds inconsistent until you notice that they do not believe those two jobs are equally hard to find.

Then show the football decisions.

Do not decorate a business memo with sports nouns.

---

# 15. OPENINGS SHOULD HAVE LIFE, NOT THEATRICS

## Too performed

> Professional basketball committed a crime against entertainment.

## Too skeletal

> 19-18. FINAL.

## Better instinct

> With George Mikan waiting on the other side, Fort Wayne coach Murray Mendenhall had a fairly straightforward problem: the more basketball his team played, the more likely it was to lose. So the Pistons spent much of the night trying to play less of it.

The material is already strange.

The prose simply lets us enjoy the strangeness.

---

# 16. ENDINGS CAN CONNECT WITHOUT BECOMING MAXIMS

## Too neat

> Ultimately, the NBA solved the problem by changing the incentives of intelligent competition.

## Better instinct

> Pollard never had to agree that the new game was smarter. The league only had to decide which version it wanted to keep putting in front of people.

This still interprets, but it stays inside the actual dispute.

Another story may end on a quote, image, object, score, or action instead.

Do not make this ending shape a template.

---

# 17. RHYTHM CHECK

Good prose should contain a mixture of short sentences, medium sentences, occasional longer sentences, simple clauses, connected clauses, plain statements, textured statements, direct quotes, concrete description, and occasional aside.

Avoid both staccato blocks where every sentence is eight words long and breathless sentences with four or five linked clauses and no place to rest.

A useful private test:

> Read the paragraph aloud. Does it sound like somebody talking well, or like somebody sprinting through prepared copy?

---

# 18. JEST / CHARACTER DOSAGE

Personality is seasoning, not the meal.

A good article may contain several amused observations. A serious article may contain none.

Do not joke on schedule, force a quip into every paragraph, call attention to the narrator, use sarcasm as intelligence, mock people for caring about sports, or make victims, fans, or sincere participants the butt of the joke.

The narrator is inside the world, not sneering at it.

---

# 19. FINAL PROSE TEST

Before approving prose, ask:

1. Do related thoughts connect naturally?
2. Are there enough ordinary sentences to make the colorful ones feel earned?
3. Are short sentences being used selectively rather than as fake energy?
4. Are long sentences controlled rather than breathless?
5. Does personality arise from noticing the story?
6. Are asides incidental rather than scheduled?
7. Does the narrator ever explain a fact that already landed?
8. Could the prose be read aloud without sounding mechanical?
9. Does the temperature fit the material?
10. Does the writing sound alive without trying to sound alive?

If not, revise.
