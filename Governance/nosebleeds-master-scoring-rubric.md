# NOSEBLEEDS
## Master Scoring Rubric v1.2
### Batch Two

**Purpose:** standardized evaluation of original drafts, every revision, final copy, future model versions, and future prompt-pack versions.  
**Inherits:** Editorial OS v1.0; Brand Bible v1.3; Voice Bible v0.8; Research Standard v1.2.

The rubric is an instrument, not a judge.

It produces a profile.

The EIC role produces the autonomous verdict under policy, and the human final gate retains taste veto.

A piece that satisfies the instrument and does not feel alive does not publish automatically.

---

# 1. THE SCALE

Every applicable dimension uses:

**0 to 10, half-point increments.**

A dimension is never silently omitted.

If a dimension genuinely does not apply to the assigned story mode, record:

```text
N/A
reason
```

Every score includes a concise justification tied to evidence in the article: quoted phrase, paragraph number, specific passage, specific fact, or verification report item where applicable.

A score without evidence is invalid.

## Shared anchor meanings

**10:** exemplar-level for this dimension; no meaningful improvement identified.  
**9.5:** exceptional; publication quality with nothing a strong editor is likely to change.  
**9:** excellent; one small improvement may exist.  
**8:** good; a specific meaningful improvement exists.  
**7:** adequate; does the job but would not be remarked upon.  
**6:** weak; a reader notices the shortfall.  
**5:** failing; the shortfall undermines the piece.  
**3:** badly failing.  
**0:** absent, fabricated, or actively harmful where applicable.

The aspiration is 9.5.

The publication threshold is lower than the aspiration by design.

Thresholds are calibration hypotheses, not permission to redefine a 9.0 as a 9.5.

---

# 2. THE SEVENTEEN DIMENSIONS

Grouping is for readability.

Groups do not carry mathematical weight.

---

# A. THE STORY

## A1. Hook

Does the opening give a non-feature reader a reason to continue?

**9.5:** the hook is immediate, specific, and repeatable on its own; the first paragraph makes the click feel justified; the outcome is withheld only when withholding genuinely improves the story.  
**8:** hook is clear by the end of the first paragraph but mildly delayed, softened, or over-explained.  
**7:** hook exists but the reader works to find it.  
**5:** throat clearing, setup, thesis, or biography arrives before the interesting thing.  
**3:** the opening could open almost any sports feature.

## A2. Story

Is there a real story rather than an explainer or fact collection?

**9.5:** a specific narrative hinge or bounded sequence creates a visible before and after; the human carrier is alive on the page; aftermath matters; the piece could not be reduced to a reference entry.  
**8:** all major elements exist, one is thin.  
**7:** real story, but hinge or human carrier is soft.  
**5:** explainer or retelling.  
**3:** list of facts.

## A3. Human stakes

Did real people experience something consequential, and does the piece make that concrete?

**9.5:** a named person or richly documented collective carries specific consequences that are shown rather than announced.  
**8:** stakes are real and specific but partly told.  
**7:** consequences are concrete but human texture is thin.  
**5:** stakes are asserted at group or institutional level.  
**3:** nobody in particular experiences anything.

A collective may score fully when the collective experience is the actual subject. Do not penalize a story for refusing to invent a protagonist.

## A4. Discovery

Does a lifelong fan learn something load-bearing?

**9.5:** at least one Verified or properly Attributed discovery most fans do not know materially changes the story.  
**8:** real discovery, but decorative or less central.  
**7:** known material seen from an unfamiliar side.  
**5:** retelling.  
**3:** everything is in the standard summary.

## A5. Originality

Relative to the standard telling in the Research Packet:

**9.5:** departs materially in hook, human carrier, source, finding, perspective, or claim calibration; or demonstrates that the standard telling is wrong.  
**8:** distinct conception using familiar material.  
**7:** familiar conception with better execution.  
**5:** standard telling.  
**3:** derivative of a specific existing treatment.

---

# B. THE READING EXPERIENCE

## B1. Momentum

Does the piece keep earning the next paragraph?

**9.5:** no meaningful stall; background appears when the reader has formed the question it answers; every section creates pull.  
**8:** one soft patch.  
**7:** one real stall that a motivated reader pushes through.  
**5:** sections exist because the packet contained material.  
**3:** the reader stops.

## B2. Structure and flow

Is the architecture right for this story and largely invisible?

**9.5:** the order serves the hinge; transitions feel like the next necessary fact; section headers, if used, help; nothing important is preempted or misplaced.  
**8:** one structural improvement exists.  
**7:** shape works but shows.  
**5:** context or chronology is in the wrong place; turn is buried or preempted.  
**3:** the piece fights its own order.

## B3. Accessibility

Could someone who does not follow this sport feel the stakes without being treated like an outsider?

**9.5:** stakes become legible early; specialized knowledge is explained only when needed and in the smallest useful amount.  
**8:** one explanation is slightly early, long, or technical.  
**7:** a setup paragraph is required but remains readable.  
**5:** assumes insider knowledge.  
**3:** insider-only.

## B4. Entertainment

Is the article a pleasure to read in the register it chose?

Entertainment does not mean comedy.

It can mean delight, dread, awe, tension, tenderness, fascination, or irresistible curiosity.

**9.5:** the reader would willingly read it twice or immediately send it; the chosen pleasure is sustained.  
**8:** enjoyable throughout.  
**7:** fine.  
**5:** dutiful.  
**3:** homework.

---

# C. THE VOICE

## C1. Voice

Does it sound like a fan who did the reading, in the stands, in this story's register?

**9.5:** unmistakably Nosebleeds and unmistakably this story; no interchangeable profundity; narrator recedes when the material needs room; mascot personality absent from body copy.  
**8:** one clear drift such as a professorial paragraph, hype sentence, or performative line.  
**7:** correct but generic; could be a good feature anywhere.  
**5:** wrong posture.  
**3:** another publication.

## C2. Prose

Sentence-level quality.

Does rhythm follow thought?

Are ordinary sentences allowed?

Is the prose textured without sounding synthetic?

**9.5:** sounds written; sentence lengths and paragraph shapes vary naturally; no device becomes a beat; no hard-prohibited construction; no closing swell.  
**8:** one noticeable tic.  
**7:** competent with detectable cadence.  
**5:** recognizable AI rhythm, prestige mannerism, or trailer copy.  
**3:** prose repeatedly calls attention to its own machinery.

Watchlist phrases from Voice Bible v0.8 create review flags.

They are not automatic failures unless the phrase itself violates a hard prohibition.

## C3. Restore uncertainty

For applicable modes, do the people in the story know only what they knew then, and does the story reproduce the actual expectation of the time?

**9.5:** no hindsight contamination; contemporaneous belief is concrete; the plausible alternative is legible where one genuinely existed; the piece does not manufacture doubt.  
**8:** one mild hindsight leak or underused contemporaneous source.  
**7:** reconstruction exists but outcome leaks or present-day knowledge intrudes.  
**5:** hindsight throughout.  
**3:** the piece is fundamentally narrated backward from what we know now.

### Inevitability mode

When the Research Packet marks `inevitability_mode`, full marks do **not** require a fake alternative outcome.

A 9.5 instead requires contemporaneous certainty accurately reconstructed, no manufactured suspense, genuine tension found inside the certainty, and correct use of the Belief File.

N/A is allowed only where the Research Standard logged a valid reduced or skipped mode.

---

# D. THE PAYOFF

## D1. Emotional payoff

Does the article land in its chosen register without explaining the feeling after the fact?

**9.5:** the turn, reaction, detail, or consequence produces the response on its own.  
**8:** lands with one moment of telling.  
**7:** lands softly.  
**5:** emotion is announced rather than produced.  
**3:** nothing lands.

A funny piece is not penalized for failing to become sentimental.

A serious piece is not rewarded for decorative sorrow.

## D2. Sports magic

Internal label only.

Does the article make the reader glad sports exist, or intensely aware of why people care about them?

**9.5:** sport is alive on the page through play, competition, bodies, objects, ritual, fans, strategy, stakes, or another story-appropriate form; every outward thread returns to the game; the reader feels the meaning without being told.  
**8:** sport is alive, but one adjacent thread pulls too far.  
**7:** sport is present but the piece could partly migrate to another subject.  
**5:** sport is mostly setting.  
**3:** the article explains why sports matter instead of making the reader feel it.

A crowd is not required.

## D3. Ending

Does the piece end where the story ends?

**9.5:** final image, action, quote, fact, observation, or earned interpretation; removing the final paragraph would make the piece worse.  
**8:** strong ending with one excess sentence or slight reach.  
**7:** works but reaches.  
**5:** closing swell, summary, lesson, or generic universal.  
**3:** paragraph about what sports mean.

## D4. Contagion

What escapes the article?

**9.5:** at least two strong contagious units, such as a fact and an image, number and argument, or feeling and line; at least one can be repeated in one sentence.  
**8:** one strong unit.  
**7:** shareable mainly inside the sport's fanbase.  
**5:** little escapes.  
**3:** reader cannot clearly say what the article was about.

The headline may itself become one contagious unit, but is not required to.

---

# E. THE TRUTH

## E1. Factual confidence

E1 is **computed deterministically from the verification report** for the current article version.

The cold scorer does not invent or reinterpret the E1 score. It confirms that the verification report is present, internally consistent, and attached to the same article version.

Revision history is invisible to E1.

The verification report emits these counts:

```text
fabrication_count
invented_source_count
invented_quote_count
prohibited_claim_asserted_count

external_load_bearing_claim_count
load_bearing_state_mismatch_count
load_bearing_material_error_count

non_load_bearing_external_unresolved_count
non_load_bearing_state_mismatch_count
non_load_bearing_material_error_count

quote_verbatim_mismatch_count
statistic_mismatch_count

metadata_only_warning_count
```

The score is computed in this order, with the most severe applicable band governing.

**0.0** if any of the following is greater than zero:

- `fabrication_count`
- `invented_source_count`
- `invented_quote_count`
- `prohibited_claim_asserted_count`

**3.0** if `external_load_bearing_claim_count > 0`.

**5.0** if any load-bearing claim is materially wrong or stated at the wrong epistemic state:

- `load_bearing_state_mismatch_count > 0`
- `load_bearing_material_error_count > 0`

**7.0** if there are three or more substantive non-load-bearing issues, or two or more unresolved external non-load-bearing claims.

**8.0** if there are two substantive non-load-bearing issues, or one unresolved external non-load-bearing claim.

**9.0** if there is exactly one substantive non-load-bearing issue.

Substantive non-load-bearing issues include state mismatch, material factual error, quote-verbatim mismatch, and statistic mismatch.

**9.5** if there are zero substantive article-text issues and at most one `metadata_only_warning_count`.

**10.0** if every count above is zero.

A metadata-only warning is a verification-record or source-display cleanup that does not change the truth, wording, attribution, number, quote, or epistemic calibration of article prose.

If a category is ambiguous, the verification controller must classify it before E1 is emitted. The cold scorer does not decide what is "trivial."

A revised article version receives a new verification report and a newly computed E1 based only on that version.

# 3. PUBLICATION GATES

A gate failure cannot be offset by strength elsewhere.

## Gate 1: Truth

**E1 must be 9.5 or above.**

E1 is supplied by the deterministic verification report, not by either cold scorer.

No unresolved load-bearing caveat.

No Prohibited Claim used as fact.

Any fabrication is an automatic 0 and terminal failure for that run.

## Gate 2: Hook

**A1 must be 8.0 or above.**

## Gate 3: Story

**A2 must be 8.0 or above.**

## Gate 4: Human stakes

**A3 must be 7.5 or above.**

## Gate 5: Sport

**D2 must be 7.0 or above**, and the EIC must judge that the piece passes the different-industry test.

## Gate 6: Entertainment

**B4 must be 8.0 or above.**

Correct, rigorous, and boring is not publication-ready.

## Gate 7: Hard prohibitions

Hard-fail until fixed for:

- em dash in article prose
- first-person singular in article prose
- fabricated access, dialogue, interiority, or reporting
- templated contrast specifically prohibited by the Voice Bible
- mascot voice inside article body
- calls to action or pasted-on morals inside narrative prose

### Review flags, not automatic failures

The following are surfaced to C2 or B2 review rather than mechanically failed:

- synthetic-phrase watchlist hits
- repeated one-sentence paragraphs
- repeated fragments
- rhetorical-question reliance
- bullet or list formatting
- bold run-in labels
- repeated headline syntax

The scanner may find a suspicious phrase.

It does not decide whether a non-hard phrase is guilty.

## Gate 8: Legal

No open `legal_review_flag` on a load-bearing living-subject claim.

The human final gate clears the flag.

## Gate 9: Length

Within target format band or the EIC records a specific exception.

Length is subordinate to the story, but exceptions must be explicit.

# 4. VERDICT

No arithmetic mean decides publication.

The profile does.

For ledger reporting, a display mean may still be shown, but it has no authority over gates or verdict.

## PUBLISH

Requires all of the following:

1. All nine gates pass.
2. No applicable dimension is below 7.0.
3. All five Story dimensions, A1 through A5, are 8.5 or above.
4. Entertainment B4 is 8.5 or above.
5. Voice C1 and Prose C2 are 8.5 or above.
6. Sports Magic D2 and Ending D3 are 8.0 or above.
7. Factual Confidence E1 is 9.5 or above.
8. At least 60 percent of applicable dimensions are 9.0 or above.
9. The EIC taste judgment is affirmative: **the piece sings**.

For the 60 percent rule, always round the required count **up**:

```text
required_9_plus_dimensions =
  ceil(applicable_dimension_count * 0.60)
```

Examples:

- 17 applicable dimensions -> 11 at 9.0+
- 16 applicable dimensions -> 10 at 9.0+
- 15 applicable dimensions -> 9 at 9.0+

The EIC records one sentence explaining what makes the piece worth publishing.

The 60 percent rule is a calibration hypothesis, not a hidden average. It prevents a technically gate-clean piece with many merely adequate dimensions from publishing while giving more flexibility than a fixed "12 of 17" count.

## REVISE

When truth and legal gates are fixable, editorial gates fail on dimensions that revision can plausibly move, or the profile does not meet PUBLISH.

The EIC revision brief names dimensions that must move, passages causing the problem, facts or passages that must not be damaged, target register, and whether full or targeted revision is required.

## KILL

When load-bearing truth failure cannot be resolved; Story gate fails because no real story exists; Sport gate fails and the piece has left orbit; living-subject exposure cannot be cleared; a second substantive revision does not improve targeted failures; or the EIC taste veto determines the piece is dead despite compliance.

A kill is the correct output when the story is not good enough.

## Reporting-only Nosebleeds Score

For the ledger, display the unweighted mean of applicable dimensions, rounded to the nearest half point.

It is a summary only.

A piece can display 9.2 and fail Hook.

The profile and gates govern.

---

# 5. MODE AND FORMAT ADJUSTMENTS

The dimensions do not change by format.

Anchors are interpreted against the format.

## Quick Story

B2 Structure is judged against a single-turn shape.

C3 may be N/A when the Research Standard logged a valid skip.

A4 Discovery may be the hook itself.

D4 Contagion should usually be strong because a Quick has little reason to exist if the premise does not travel.

## Core Story

All dimensions scored unless Research Standard mode explicitly permits C3 N/A.

## Big Story

B1 Momentum and B2 Structure are read more strictly, not more generously.

Length increases the burden.

## N/A handling

Every N/A requires a reason.

N/A is excluded from the 60 percent rule denominator.

It may not be used to avoid a low score.

# 6. SCORING PROTOCOL

## Cold

The official scorer sees the immutable article version, Research Packet, source ledger, verification report, and assigned story mode and register.

For A1 through D4, the scorer assigns scores normally. For E1, it validates and imports the deterministic score emitted by the verification report.

It does not see prior scores, forensic reviewer reports, EIC brief, revision history, or prior verdict.

Blindness is enforced by the context matrix.

## Evidence-required

Every score cites the article or verification record.

## Same rubric every time

The same rubric scores ORIGINAL, REVISED_01, REVISED_02, and FINAL.

Forensic specialists diagnose.

They may recommend scores in their specialty.

The official cold scorer produces the ledger score.

## Versioned

Every score record includes:

```text
rubric_version
scorer_model_id
article_version
prompt_pack_version
scored_at
```

Missing version metadata invalidates the record.

## Delta reporting

After each revision, record per dimension:

```text
previous_score
current_score
delta
targeted_by_eic: yes | no
```

Also record whether targeted dimensions improved, whether protected dimensions regressed, and whether untargeted dimensions moved unexpectedly.

## Dual scorers at launch

Until calibration stabilizes, two cold scorers from different model families score each version on the judgment dimensions A1 through D4.

E1 is excluded from dual-scorer disagreement because it is computed by verification.

A disagreement of 1.5 or more on any other gating dimension is flagged.

Under the conservative launch policy, the lower gating score governs the gate.

If repeated patterns emerge, recalibrate anchors or scorer prompts rather than averaging away the disagreement.

---

# 7. TASTE VETO AND HUMAN FINAL GATE

A rubric can miss dead competence.

The EIC role and human final gate may reject a piece that meets every numeric threshold.

They must record why in one sentence.

Useful reasons include:

- does not sound alive
- no real moment
- correct and nobody would send it
- too constructed
- technically good but emotionally inert
- feels like a demonstration of the system

These veto notes are high-value calibration data.

The human may also publish a piece the rubric calls REVISE.

Every override is logged:

```text
model_verdict
human_verdict
rationale
```

The override log feeds future calibration and the Tier D voice corpus.

---

# 8. VISUAL READINESS IS DOWNSTREAM, NOT AN ARTICLE SCORE

Visual readiness is deliberately excluded from the seventeen-dimension article profile.

A brilliant article should not receive a lower article score because the human has not yet sourced the right photograph.

Batch Three's Publication Package and Visual Media OS must add a separate final-package status:

```text
visual_readiness:
  VISUAL_READY
  VISUAL_NEEDS_HUMAN
  VISUAL_BLOCKED
```

That downstream check should evaluate hero recommendation, inline visual placements, source links actually found, visible credit information, what each visual proves or shows or explains, AI-media prompts where interpretation or explanation is preferable to documentary imagery, and unresolved sourcing or rights questions surfaced to the human gate.

Visual readiness can block publication package completion without altering the article-quality profile.

---

# 9. LEARNING FROM SCORES

Join idea profile, research outcome, article profile, revision deltas, final human decision, and measured reader behavior when available.

Questions the ledger should eventually answer:

- Which discovery lanes produce PUBLISH most often?
- Which idea profiles create the strongest Story and Discovery scores?
- Which registers consistently underperform on Prose?
- Do Hook scores predict click or share behavior?
- Does Contagion predict forwarding?
- Which reviewers identify problems that revisions actually fix?
- Which revision prompts improve Momentum without damaging Voice?
- Which model families disagree systematically?
- Which research weaknesses later become factual or narrative weaknesses?

Rubric amendments come from this evidence and the override log.

Not from theory alone.

        fabrication_count

# CHANGE LOG

## v1.2

- Made E1 Factual Confidence deterministic from verification-report counts rather than scorer judgment.
- Removed E1 from dual-scorer disagreement and required cold scorers only to validate and import the verification score.
- Defined the 60 percent PUBLISH rule with explicit ceiling rounding and implementation examples.
- Added a downstream Visual Readiness requirement for Batch Three's Publication Package rather than adding visuals to the article-quality profile.
- Preserved the distinction between hard drafting prohibitions and reviewer flags for bullets, lists, bold labels, cadence, and watchlist phrases.
- Updated inheritance to Research Standard v1.2.

## v1.1

- Added an Entertainment publication gate.
- Raised the Truth gate to E1 9.5 and rewrote E1 as a current-version-only score.
- Reconciled the Prohibitions Gate with Voice Bible v0.8: hard bans fail mechanically; watchlist phrases and cadence flags go to review.
- Removed bullets, lists, and bold labels from automatic hard failure.
- Made Sports Magic crowd-independent.
- Added inevitability-mode handling to Restore Uncertainty.
- Replaced the fixed "twelve dimensions at 9+" rule with a 60 percent applicable-dimension calibration rule plus stronger core-dimension requirements.
- Preserved cold scoring, dual scorers at launch, evidence-required justifications, delta reporting, taste veto, human overrides, and ledger learning.
