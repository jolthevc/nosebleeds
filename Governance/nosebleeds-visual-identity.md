# NOSEBLEEDS
## Visual Identity
### Canonical launch design brief

This document governs the visible Nosebleeds brand system: color, typography, logo usage, article-image treatment, and the distinction between ordinary editorial art and packaged promotional assets. Story-image sourcing and rights remain governed by the Visual Media OS.

## 1. Brand posture

Nosebleeds should feel like a great old sports annual discovered in a box under the bleachers, then rebuilt with modern editorial taste. Warm, fan-first, collectible, slightly irreverent, and premium without becoming precious. The brand is in the stands, not the press box.

The visual identity should carry the same tension as the name and slogan: the word **Nosebleeds** is cheap-seat, physical, and self-aware; **“The magical stories behind the games we love.”** is sincere.

## 2. Core palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary navy | Deep Navy | `#01274E` | Wordmark, titles, rules, primary UI |
| Shadow navy | Midnight Navy | `#00193D` | Depth, dark fields, logo shadow |
| Primary red | Athletic Red | `#B91619` | Accent, status, emphasis, logo shadow |
| Paper | Warm Cream | `#FCF3DB` | Editorial background, cards, print feel |
| Gold | Dark Golden Yellow | `#E8A84E` | Star, small highlights, selected accents |
| Neutral | White | `#FFFFFF` | Negative space and contrast |

Natural supporting colors are allowed inside photographs and illustrations. The palette is a brand bias, not a rule that every image must be recolored into five inks.

## 3. Typography

Launch defaults:

- **Display / article title:** Roboto Slab, Bold or ExtraBold. Sturdy, retro-sporting, not novelty varsity type. Default color: Primary Navy.
- **Subtitle / dek:** Source Serif 4, Regular or Semibold. May use italics for a short phrase when the sentence earns it; do not italicize the entire dek by default. Default color: Shadow Navy or Primary Navy at reduced emphasis.
- **Body:** Source Serif 4, Regular. Comfortable editorial measure, generous leading, dark navy/near-black text on Warm Cream or White.
- **Eyebrows / labels / metadata / UI:** Barlow Condensed, Semibold. Uppercase or small caps where useful. Use Athletic Red, Primary Navy, or neutral text according to hierarchy.

Text styling rules:

- Underlines are functional, primarily for links. They are not a decorative house tic.
- Italics are for natural editorial emphasis, titles of works, and occasional tonal stress.
- Bold inside article body is rare. Do not turn prose into a deck of highlighted takeaways.
- Avoid all-caps body copy.
- Avoid glossy sports-broadcast typography and excessive varsity/college novelty fonts.

## 4. Logo system

The **primary banner wordmark** is the normal masthead. The **condensed/square-ish wordmark** is used where the full horizontal banner is awkward. The **circular badge** is secondary and reserved for avatars, stamps, small-format social applications, and other special cases.

The gold star is a recognizable secondary motif. It may appear independently in small brand surfaces when the full mark would be noisy.

Lou may appear as the visual mascot/byline device in approved brand surfaces. Lou is not required in the article-image system and is never inserted into evidence-bearing visuals or the hero simply to brand the image. No separate Lou voice module exists in the runtime.

## 5. Article-image house treatment

The primary Nosebleeds article treatment is **painterly gouache editorial illustration grounded in a real sports image**, with:

- subtle ink-line definition
- restrained halftone / print texture
- warm paper character
- deep navy, faded athletic red, warm cream, white, and dark golden yellow as a palette bias
- restrained natural supporting color
- realistic, recognizable figures and action
- preservation of the source image's actual person, moment, action, composition, and era cues
- premium retro sports annual / classic all-star program / collectible print-media sensibility

The treatment should not become abstract, surreal, overly cartoonish, glossy, fantasy-like, or a cheap nostalgia filter. Anatomy, uniforms, equipment, signage, setting, and period details must not be invented or casually altered.

A sourced transformation remains subject to the rights, credit, licensing, and permission considerations of the underlying image. Stylization is not a rights workaround.

## 6. Visual modes

### Hero Art

Strongest painterly execution and cleanest composition. No baked-in Nosebleeds logo. No heavy designed border. A subtle cream margin, paper edge, or print character is acceptable when it serves the image.

### Inline Editorial

Slightly simpler and more restrained than the hero. Its first job is still to prove, show, or explain.

### Social Card

May add title lockup, logo, border, star, and stronger brand framing. Packaging can be more explicit because the asset itself is promotional.

### Cover / Marquee

May use full title, designed frame, wordmark, star, and stronger retro-program language.

### Secondary visual styles

Vintage-poster treatment may be used for big-event promotion or special issues. Retro news illustration / collage is useful for mythology, documents, archives, and record-versus-legend stories. Screenprint / heavier halftone may appear occasionally for gritty stories. These are supporting modes, not the default hero identity.

## 7. Hard negatives

- No fake archival photography or synthetic documentary moment.
- No surreal/fantasy treatment for ordinary editorial stories.
- No childish cartoon treatment.
- No distorted anatomy or invented props, uniforms, signage, or historical details.
- No generic glossy sports-ad look.
- No loud logo stamping on ordinary article art.
- No heavy border on ordinary article art.
- No over-texturing that makes the image muddy or illegible.
- No decorative visual that neither proves, shows, nor explains.

## 8. Final test

A reader should be able to see an unbranded Nosebleeds hero and begin to recognize the publication from the treatment alone. The identity comes from accumulated taste, not from putting a logo on every image.
