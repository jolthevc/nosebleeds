# NOSEBLEEDS
## Editorial Calibration Examples v1.0
### Examples teach taste. They do not define the menu.

**Status:** Runtime calibration companion for Ideation, Research, and Conception  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate what Nosebleeds means by appeal before novelty, find the interesting version, and let sports open into the rest of the world without losing the sport.

---

# 0. HOW TO USE THIS FILE

These are calibration examples, not templates.

Do not:
- copy these subjects merely because they appear here
- reproduce their structures mechanically
- force every piece to contain business, psychology, culture, or mythology
- search for analogues to these examples instead of searching the sports world freely
- assume these are the only valid kinds of Nosebleeds pieces

Do:
- study the editorial move each example makes
- notice that familiar material is welcome
- notice that the deeper layer changes from piece to piece
- notice that the sport remains essential
- notice that the conception is more specific than the topic
- notice that the best version is usually richer than recap but less academic than an explainer

The lesson is not:

> Find another 28-3.

The lesson is:

> Start with something people care about, find the most interesting way into it, and make every additional layer increase the pleasure of the sports story.

# 1. FAMOUS GAME: 28-3

## Surface

The Patriots erase a 28-3 deficit against Atlanta in Super Bowl LI.

## Weak version

A chronological recap of the comeback with familiar highlights and a conclusion that Tom Brady is great.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Reconstruct the collapse through the decisions that kept giving New England oxygen: Atlanta's offensive choices, the changing value of aggression once the lead became enormous, the Patriots' shrinking margin for error, and the strange psychological asymmetry between a team trying to finish a win and a team with nothing left to protect.

The football remains the spine.

The piece can also show how the comeback hardened the mythology of Brady as inevitable and turned one scoreboard image into shorthand for a particular kind of sporting dread.

## Why it works

The reader gets the game they came for, but the game becomes more legible, tense, and meaningful.

The deeper layer is not a psychology lecture.

It explains what the viewer was watching.

# 2. FAMOUS PERFORMANCE: THE FLU GAME

## Surface

Michael Jordan scores 38 points in Game 5 of the 1997 NBA Finals while visibly ill.

## Weak version

Jordan was sick, Jordan was heroic, Jordan was the greatest.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Reconstruct what was actually known about Jordan's condition that night, how his body and game changed possession by possession, what teammates and opponents believed in real time, and how later retellings altered the story.

Follow the famous image of Scottie Pippen holding Jordan off the floor and ask why this particular performance became such a durable piece of Jordan mythology.

## Why it works

The subject is famous because people care.

Nosebleeds does not need to pretend otherwise.

The reason to retell is the combination of reconstruction, myth, memory, physical performance, and one unforgettable image.

Admiration is earned through detail rather than declared through hero worship.

# 3. TEAM / EXECUTIVE: LES SNEAD AND THE RAMS

## Surface

The Rams have remained unusually competitive in a league designed to drag successful teams back toward the middle.

## Weak version

A business article explaining that Les Snead manages the roster like an investor.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Follow the Rams' evolving theory of football value.

Snead and the organization have repeatedly treated some positions and proven stars as scarce premium assets while accepting churn, youth, and cheaper replacement elsewhere.

Trace that philosophy through draft capital, blockbuster trades, contracts, the Super Bowl roster, players the Rams chose not to pay, the famous "F them picks" era, and the later return to developing waves of young contributors.

The investing analogy can illuminate the philosophy, but the piece is ultimately about how the Rams decide what a football player is worth.

## Why it works

Business and capital allocation explain actual football decisions.

The commercial layer does not pull the reader away from the game.

It makes roster construction more interesting to a fan.

This is a key Nosebleeds model for Business, Commercial, Strategy, Owner, Front Office, and Organization pieces.

# 4. PROFILE: TIGER WOODS THROUGH WHAT CHANGED AROUND HIM

## Surface

A profile of Tiger Woods.

## Weak version

Child prodigy. Stanford. Masters. Major championships. Scandal. Injuries. Comeback. Legacy.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Understand Tiger partly through what happened to everyone and everything around him.

Opponents heard roars from other holes and knew what they meant. Television audiences changed when he entered contention. Golf courses were lengthened. Training expectations shifted. Purses grew. Nike made a massive bet. A generation of players grew up treating athleticism and intimidation differently.

His own triumphs, failures, injuries, relationships, and reinventions remain essential, but the profile gains shape by following the force he exerted on the sport around him.

## Why it works

It is a profile without becoming Wikipedia.

Greatness is demonstrated through consequence.

The subject can be admired while remaining complicated and human.

# 5. FAN PHENOMENON: FANTASY FOOTBALL

## Surface

Millions of people play fantasy football.

## Weak version

A history of who invented fantasy football and how participation grew.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Tell the story of the hobby that taught millions of NFL fans to root for outcomes that conflict with their own teams.

Follow that contradiction into Sunday viewing habits, statistical obsession, injury information, RedZone, group chats, friendships, player awareness, media coverage, and the transformation of otherwise meaningless late-game touches into events with emotional stakes.

## Why it works

A familiar pastime becomes a story about how football fandom itself changed.

Culture, media, technology, and business can all appear, but they are woven through the lived experience of watching football.

# 6. FAMOUS TEAM: THE DREAM TEAM

## Surface

The 1992 United States men's Olympic basketball team overwhelms the field in Barcelona.

## Weak version

A roster of Hall of Famers dominates inferior competition.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Tell Barcelona partly through the surreal experience of opponents who wanted photographs with the players they were supposed to beat.

Then follow the contradiction: a tournament with very little competitive suspense became one of the most consequential basketball spectacles ever.

The games, personalities, practices, celebrity, international reaction, and commercial aura can lead naturally into basketball's globalization and the modern idea of the athlete as a worldwide entertainment figure.

## Why it works

The lack of suspense becomes part of the story rather than a weakness.

Basketball, celebrity, commerce, and globalization are one tapestry.

---

# 7. ORGANIZATION PROFILE: THE NBA

## Surface

A profile of the NBA.

## Weak version

A chronological history from league formation through today's stars.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Explore how the NBA became unusually good at selling people as much as teams.

Move through the league's moments of reinvention, Magic and Bird, Jordan, sneakers, David Stern, television, Kobe, LeBron, player empowerment, social media, and the modern star-driven league.

Ask what the NBA gained from making individual personality so central, what tensions it created, and why basketball proved unusually compatible with celebrity culture.

## Why it works

An enormous institution becomes manageable through one defining characteristic.

The conception allows history, media, business, culture, labor, and actual basketball to coexist without becoming a textbook.

# 8. CHAMPIONSHIP / CITY: CLEVELAND 2016

## Surface

The Cavaliers come back from 3-1 against the 73-win Warriors to win the 2016 NBA Finals.

## Weak version

A recap of Games 5, 6, and 7 followed by a statement that the championship meant a lot to Cleveland.

## Stronger Nosebleeds conception

Tell the Finals as both an extraordinary basketball comeback and the release of decades of accumulated civic sports misery.

LeBron's departure and return, Cleveland's history of near-misses, Golden State's 73 wins, Draymond Green's suspension, The Block, The Shot, the final defensive stand, and the celebration all belong.

The city is not a separate sociology section.

Its history changes the emotional weight of every possession.

## Why it works

Sometimes the deeper layer is simply what the game meant to the people watching it.

No hidden business mechanism or contrarian thesis is required.

# 9. WHAT THESE EXAMPLES ARE CALIBRATING

Across these examples, the system should learn:

## Appeal before novelty

28-3, Jordan, Tiger, the NBA, and the Dream Team are not weaknesses because they are famous.

Fame is stored audience interest.

## Conception before topic

"Profile Tiger Woods" is a topic.

"Tiger Woods through what changed around him" is a conception.

## The sport stays in the piece

The Rams story still needs football decisions.

The fantasy story still needs Sundays.

The Cleveland story still needs the Finals.

The Dream Team story still needs basketball.

## Deeper does not mean more academic

Psychology should explain a collapse, not become a lecture.

Finance should explain roster choices, not become an investing primer.

Culture should change how the reader experiences the sporting subject, not become detached commentary.

## Multiple dimensions are allowed

A piece can contain competition, character, business, media, culture, money, psychology, and memory.

The final article should feel like one tapestry rather than a stack of sections.

## Familiar stories need a reason to retell

They do not need a secret.

The reason may be:
- reconstruction
- perspective
- synthesis
- consequence
- contradiction
- mythology
- a neglected participant
- a better explanatory frame
- a richer profile conception
- simply a much better telling

## Pure sport remains valid

Not every piece needs to "be about" something outside sports.

Sometimes the additional value is better reconstruction, sharper decisions, richer character, or the emotional meaning already inside the game.

# 10. ANTI-COPY RULE

Before using these examples, explicitly remember:

> **Calibration examples demonstrate editorial moves, not preferred subjects or required structures.**

A strong slate should not look like eight remixes of this file.

Search freely.

Use taste.

The final question remains:

> **If Nosebleeds made this extremely well, would a lot of sports fans be glad it exists?**


# CALIBRATION: SAME SUBJECT, BETTER ARTICLE

These examples demonstrate post-research reframing.

They are not preferred subjects and are not templates.

The lesson is that the nominal subject may be less interesting than the human, competitive, commercial, cultural, or absurd problem inside it.

## Shot clock

Weaker conception:

> A history of how the NBA invented the 24-second shot clock.

Why weaker:

It invites chronology and explanation. The Writer can produce a completely correct article without ever finding the most entertaining thing.

Stronger possible conceptions:

> Basketball accidentally created a game where the smartest thing a winning team could do was stop playing basketball.

> The NBA eventually had to outlaw safety because perfectly rational teams were making professional basketball miserable to watch.

> Start with 19-18 and treat the score like a crime scene: how did a professional sport reach the point where refusing to shoot made competitive sense?

The shot clock remains central. It becomes the answer inside a larger story rather than the encyclopedia subject.

## 28-3

Weaker conception:

> The story of the Patriots' historic Super Bowl comeback.

Stronger possible conceptions:

> Anatomy of losing a game that had become almost impossible to lose.

> The night "28-3" stopped being a score and became a cultural synonym for collapse.

The event remains the spine, but the conception determines what the reader notices.

## Tiger Woods

Weaker conception:

> The career and legacy of Tiger Woods.

Stronger possible conception:

> What it was actually like to play professional golf while Tiger Woods changed the meaning of a tournament around you.

This reveals Tiger through other people's decisions, fear, economics, crowds, television, and competitive experience rather than résumé chronology.

## Les Snead / Rams

Weaker conception:

> How the Rams build their roster.

Stronger possible conception:

> How one NFL team decided that some football players are worth paying almost anything for and others should be replaced before they become expensive.

The business layer works because it explains actual football behavior.

## The governing move

Before locking a conception, ask:

> Are we writing about the nominal subject because it is truly the best story, or because it is the easiest thing to organize?

The best Nosebleeds version often keeps the same research territory while changing what the article is really about.
